Tango & Piazzolla · 7 min read

The Quinteto Nuevo Tango line-up

Bandoneon, violin, piano, electric guitar, bass. Why Piazzolla settled on five players in 1960, what each one does, and why the balance was hard to beat.

2026-09-18

Short answer

Piazzolla's Quinteto Nuevo Tango, formed in 1960, used bandoneon, violin, piano, electric guitar and double bass. The five cover melody, counterline, harmony, percussive drive and bass with no doubling, which let Piazzolla write genuine counterpoint while keeping a rhythm section that could still hit hard.

Why five, and why then

Piazzolla had already tried bigger. His Octeto Buenos Aires of 1955 had eight players, including two bandoneons and a string section, and it was a deliberate provocation aimed at the traditional orquesta tipica. He also ran a string orchestra in New York in the early 1960s and disliked what it did to his music. The problem with weight is that it smooths things out, and his writing depends on edges.

The Quinteto Nuevo Tango, formed in 1960, was the solution: one player per function, no doubling, nowhere to hide. He returned to the format for his second quintet from 1978 to 1988, which most listeners consider the definitive version of his music. Between them the two quintets account for the majority of the recordings people know.

What each player does

Bandoneon. Piazzolla's own instrument, and it holds the lead voice, the harmonic filling and a great deal of the rhythmic attack. It is the only instrument in the group that can do all three at once, which is why it sits at the centre rather than out front.

Violin. The second melodic voice, and the one that makes the counterpoint audible — two lines in different timbres can be followed simultaneously in a way that two bandoneons cannot. It also supplies the percussion effects: chicharra, latigo, strikes with the wood of the bow.

Piano. Harmony, but chiefly as a rhythm instrument. In this group the piano plays short, dry, hard chords on the beat much more often than it plays sustained texture, and it anchors the marcato with the bass.

Electric guitar. The contentious one, and the reason many traditionalists dismissed the group on sight. It adds a percussive middle with a clean attack, can hold chords that would muddy the piano, and takes improvised solos. It is also the sound that dates the group to its era in the best way.

Double bass. The engine. It carries the 3-3-2 and the marcato, slides into downbeats with arrastre, and slaps and strikes the instrument for percussion. In this music the bass is not accompaniment; it is the part everything else is timed against. Kicillof, Lopez Ruiz, Console and later Hector Console's long tenure in the second quintet gave the group its floor.

The arithmetic that makes it work

Count the jobs in a Piazzolla piece: a lead line, a countermelody, a harmonic bed, a percussive middle, and a bass motor. That is five jobs. The quintet assigns exactly one player to each, which means every part is exposed, and exposure is what forces the precision his rhythms need.

It also solves a balance problem. Tango's traditional orchestra has three or four bandoneons, which produces a gorgeous wall and a blurred attack — fine for dancers, fatal for counterpoint. With one of each instrument, every entry in a fugue has its own colour and can be tracked by ear. Fuga y misterio works because five distinct timbres take the subject in turn.

And it is portable. Five musicians tour, fit on a club stage, and can be paid. Piazzolla worked constantly for thirty years, and the economics of the line-up are part of why the catalogue exists.

The variants

He did not stop there. The Conjunto 9 of 1971 added a cello, viola, percussion and a second guitar and produced some of his most ambitious writing before folding for financial reasons. His electronic octets of the mid-1970s added synthesiser, electric bass and drums, and divide opinion sharply to this day. The final group, the Sexteto Nuevo Tango of 1989, dropped the violin and used two bandoneons, cello, piano, guitar and bass — a darker and more bitter sound, and the last thing he built before the stroke.

The quintet is nevertheless the format his publishers and arrangers treat as standard, and the one most ensembles reduce from or expand to.

Reducing it without losing it

If you have fewer than five players, the priority order is unforgiving: keep the bass function and one melodic voice, then add harmony. Piazzolla survives losing the guitar and even the piano. He does not survive losing the rhythmic floor, because without it the syncopations have nothing to be syncopated against.

That is the logic behind how we play it as a duo in Singapore: the double bass takes the entire rhythm-section role, including the arrastre and the percussive hits, while the accordion covers the bandoneon's melody and the piano's harmonic stabs, switching between them bar by bar. Add Ksenia and the third voice restores the counterpoint the quintet's violin was there to supply. It is a compromise, and it is an honest one, because the writing was built in independent lines and independent lines can be redistributed.

Frequently asked

What instruments were in Piazzolla's quintet?

Bandoneon, violin, piano, electric guitar and double bass. Piazzolla formed the Quinteto Nuevo Tango on that line-up in 1960 and returned to it for his second quintet from 1978 to 1988, which recorded Tango: Zero Hour and is generally regarded as the definitive group for his music.

Why did Piazzolla use an electric guitar in a tango group?

It gave him a percussive middle voice with a clean attack, chords that would have muddied the piano's register, and a player who could improvise a solo. It also signalled that this was not a traditional orchestra, which was part of the point and part of why traditionalists objected so loudly.

Can Piazzolla be played by fewer than five musicians?

Yes, and it is done constantly by duos, trios and string quartets. The writing is contrapuntal, so the lines can be redistributed. The one thing you cannot drop is the rhythmic bass function, because the syncopations only make sense against a hard, steady pulse underneath them.
